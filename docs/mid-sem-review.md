Based on the supplied notice dated 3 November 2026, registration/synopsis and review guide. Check any changes with your guide. The review is **offline on 16 or 17 November 2026**; the guide will confirm the exact time and venue. The notice asks for progress and present status, not a finished app.

## Assessment and preparation

Each is worth 10 marks (50 total, converted to 30): registration/synopsis and approved Review 1 report; literature review; group formation and individual responsibilities; objectives; and individual viva on domain, tools and technology.

- [ ] Confirm review date, time, venue, project registration and synopsis with the guide.
- [ ] Prepare a PPT of **at most 13 slides**. Use the notice's first-slide format; slide 2 lists key points. Cover introduction, objectives, literature review, materials/components, tools/software, methodology, progress and present status. Make planned versus implemented work explicit.
- [ ] Review 7–8 real papers. For each include: serial number, paper title, authors, journal/conference, publication month and year, 2–3-line summary, and remarks/key findings/tools. Split two papers per member; read and verify each paper rather than inventing findings. The supplied review guide has eight starting links.
- [ ] Fill FF-180 Review 1 with truthful completed, ongoing and remaining work. Get the guide's signatures at top and bottom, photograph it and submit the signed hard copy. The supplied registration PDF places the bottom signature on the next page: ask the guide which format to submit.
- [ ] All four attend, speak and practise explaining their own real contribution and what they learned. Confirm names and PRNs against official records; the supplied guide flags an inconsistency in Swastik's number.
- [ ] Arrange for someone from another group to take 4–5 geotagged presentation photos, including the first slide, and a one-minute video. The group leader keeps them for the later form.
- [ ] After the review, the leader emails the PPT and presentation photos to the guide using the subject format in the notice; keep the video for the later form.

## Honest evidence

Use actual commits, screenshots, design sketches and demos only when they exist. The project synopsis contains ambitious targets and statistics; verify sources before presenting them, and do not claim that planned features or fraud prevention are already achieved. Update this checklist as the team completes work.

## Starting points for literature review

The supplied preparation guide lists the following eight starting points. These are **not completed reviews**; check each publication, date and findings against the paper itself before using them.

1. Hamari, Sjöklint and Ukkonen, “The Sharing Economy: Why People Participate in Collaborative Consumption” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.23552).
2. Möhlmann, “Collaborative Consumption: Determinants of Satisfaction and the Likelihood of Using a Sharing Economy Option Again” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cb.1512).
3. ter Huurne and colleagues, “Antecedents of Trust in the Sharing Economy: A Systematic Review” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cb.1667).
4. Marth, Hartl and Penz, “Sharing on Platforms: Reducing Perceived Risk for Peer-to-Peer Platform Consumers Through Trust-Building and Regulation” — [paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821683/).
5. Zalega, “Collaborative Consumption in Consumer Behavior of Polish Young People” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.22367/jem.2018.33.08).
6. García-Rodríguez and colleagues, “New Models for Collaborative Consumption: The Role of Consumer Attitudes Among Millennials” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440221140389).
7. Neifer and colleagues, “Trust-Building in Peer-to-Peer Carsharing: Design Case Study for Algorithm-Based Reputation Systems” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10606-022-09461-4).
8. Hawlitschek, Teubner and Weinhardt, “Trust in the Sharing Economy” — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5771/0042-059X-2016-1-26).
